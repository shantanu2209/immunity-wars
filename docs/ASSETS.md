# Asset provenance

**Purpose:** record where every visual asset in this project came from. The licence question
this register was opened for is **settled — by deciding not to declare a content licence**
(Resolution log, 20 Aug 2026; reasoning in [LICENSES.md](../LICENSES.md)). The register
remains, because "where did this art come from" is a question a judge or a school may ask
**independent of licensing** — and the July art's unrecoverable provenance hole already cost
this project one decision.

---

## Why this file exists

Some of the artwork was generated with an AI image tool. Whether we may redistribute that
output under CC BY-SA 4.0 depends on that tool's terms of service **as they stood on the date
the image was generated** — terms change, and what matters is the version in force at
generation time.

We cannot answer that question from the images themselves. It has to be written down.

---

## What to record

For every asset, we need:

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Asset | Which file, and where it is used |
| Origin | Tool name and version, or "hand-drawn", or "third-party" |
| Date | When it was generated or created — determines which ToS applies |
| Account/plan | Free vs paid tiers often carry different rights |
| Prompt or source | For regeneration, and to show the work is not derived from a specific artist |
| ToS checked | Date the terms were read, and the conclusion |
| Redistribution | Yes / No / Unknown — under CC BY-SA 4.0 specifically |

## Resolution log

Record decisions here as they are made, with dates, so the reasoning survives.

**20 Aug 2026 — No content licence will be declared. Decided by Shantanu after completing the
Google Flow ToS check; this closes the question the register was opened for.**

- Google does not claim ownership of Flow output and permits commercial use on all tiers —
  so **Google is not the obstacle**, and the check that was "pending" is done.
- But "Google does not claim ownership" is not the same as "we hold a copyright we can
  license to others," and CC BY-SA works by granting rights **we hold**. Whether AI-generated
  images are copyrightable at all is unsettled law.
- Rather than resolve that ourselves, we sidestep it: **code stays Apache 2.0; game content
  stays all rights reserved.** Teachers using the app are unaffected. The only thing
  foreclosed is third-party redistribution of modified artwork — which nobody has asked for.
- Revisit only if that becomes a real need, and then **as a question for a lawyer, not for
  us.**

**2 Oct 2026 — the generated art is removed; the decision above is not changed by that.**

- Since stage L5 every picture the app ships is modelled in Blender or drawn in code here, and
  the 30 generated originals and what was built from them are out of the repository's current
  files (register, above).
- So the question the decision of 20 Aug sidestepped, whether anyone holds a copyright in
  AI-generated images, **no longer applies to anything the app ships.** It still applies to what
  is kept for reference and never shipped: the icon art in `tools/legacy/`, and the 16 rasters
  embedded in the printed A2 board.
- **No licence is declared for content, as before.** Whether to declare one now is Shantanu's to
  decide, and nothing here decides it.

**3 Oct 2026 — the Android app's icon is a generated picture; the decision of 20 Aug stands.**

- Shantanu chose an icon made with OpenAI's image model through Codex (register, above;
  `tools/art-pipeline/icon/PROVENANCE.md`). OpenAI's terms give the user the output, and assign
  OpenAI's rights in it, if any.
- So the question the decision of 20 Aug sidestepped applies again to one file the app ships.
  **No content licence is declared, as before.** The icon is not in the web app, whose screens are
  all the Clay set.
