Privacy

The Immunity Wars. In force from 3 October 2026.

The Immunity Wars is a cooperative game about how the immune system fights an infection. It was designed by Kartik Chaudhary and built under the direction of Shantanu Chaudhary, with code written with Claude (Anthropic).

In short

  • There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking of any kind.
  • Nothing about you is sold, shared or kept.
  • Playing alone sends nothing anywhere.
  • Playing together sends the name you type and your moves to the game's server, for that room only, and nothing is kept after the room closes.

What stays on your device

Your saved game, your settings (text size, and sound on or off), and whether you have finished a game, so the game knows whether to offer its guided lesson. While you are in a room played together, the device also keeps that room's code and a random value that stands for your seat, so you can go back to it if the app closes. That is forgotten when you leave the room or the game ends, and after a day in any case. Your name is not kept.

None of this is ever sent anywhere. Removing the app, or clearing this site's data in your browser, deletes all of it. The Android app does not let Android copy it into your Google account's backup.

Playing together

When you make or join a room, the app sends the game's server the name you type and the moves you make, and the server shows your name to the other players in that room. The server holds these in its memory only, to run the room. Nothing is written to disk, and a room, with everything in it, is deleted 10 minutes after its last player leaves.

The server keeps no record of who visits. Its error log holds no names and no internet addresses, and is deleted after 7 days. To stop any one connection from overwhelming it, the server counts connections by internet address, in its memory, and never writes the address down.

Use a first name or a nickname: the other players see it.

Children

Many of the game's players are children, so it is built to collect nothing about anyone, of any age. Rooms are private: they are joined only with a code a player shares, there is no way to find or meet strangers, and the only messages players can send each other are a fixed set of game phrases.

Anyone else

The game uses no outside services: its fonts and pictures are part of the app, and the web version and the server for playing together are the game's own. The Android app is distributed through Google Play, and Google's own privacy policy covers the Play Store.
